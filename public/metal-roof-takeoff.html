What's in every takeoff

Wall trim

How standing seam panel layout works

Coverage first. The coil width you roll, less the seam take-up, is the width each panel covers. That number sets the panel count on every roof side and wall, so the takeoff asks for the coil width and works the coverage out from the profile rather than taking a nominal panel width.

On the roof, panels run eave to ridge, laid across each facet from its starting edge one coverage width apart. Each panel's length is measured on the true slope of its facet. A panel that ends on a hip or valley is cut to the longest point of its run, rounded up to the inch, and trimmed to the line on the roof, so the cut list matches what goes up instead of a squares-times-waste guess. Each facet gets a numbered start marker and its panels listed in install order.

On the walls, panels run plumb from the base to the top and are laid across each wall from its left end as you face it. A panel the full width of a door or overhead door becomes a short piece over it. Panels at a window are ordered full length and cut around it on site, so there's no end lap in the wall. On a gable end or shed wall every panel is ordered at its long side, so the gable panels step up toward the peak.

Then the trim. Every edge the layout leaves needs metal: eaves, rakes, ridges, hips and valleys on the roof; corners, base, eave and gable tops and every opening on the walls. The takeoff measures each run from the same layout, so the trim matches the panels.

Trim from flat sheet

A trim part's girth (its stretch-out) is the width of flat metal it's bent from: every leg, hem and return added up. A 4 × 10 flat sheet is 48" wide, so it gives 48 divided by the girth, rounded down, pieces 10 ft long. A 12" girth gives 4 pieces a sheet and a 16" girth gives 3, so 19 pieces of a 12" eave trim take 5 sheets.

On a roof takeoff, every trim line is drawn as the part the shop bends, to your pitch and seam height: drip edge and cleat, rake, ridge and hip cap, Z-closure, W-valley and offset cleat, headwall and step flashing. Each one comes with its girth, pieces per sheet and the sheets the order takes, and the trim map shows which edge each part goes on. On a wall takeoff the trim is counted in 10 ft sticks, run by run, with 6" laps where a run is longer than a stick.

Checked against the report before you order

An EagleView report prints its own totals: the number of facets, the roof area, and the length of every eave, rake, ridge, hip and valley. The takeoff rebuilds the roof in 3D from the report's diagrams, lays the panels on that roof, and then compares what it built with those printed totals. Each comparison shows as pass or fail.

When the checks pass, the cut lengths come from a roof that agrees with the measurement. When they don't, you still get the takeoff, but it's marked not verified on screen and stamped on every page of the PDF, and the RoofCoil team is told so the reader can be fixed. A wrong number caught on screen costs nothing; a wrong number caught on the roof costs a reorder.

Frequently asked

What is a metal roof takeoff?

A metal roof takeoff is the list of everything it takes to build the roof, worked out from its measurements: how many panels and each one's cut length, plus the trim, clips, fasteners, closures, sealants and underlayment that go with them. On a standing seam roof the panels have to be laid out facet by facet, because every hip and valley changes the cut lengths. A sheet metal takeoff for metal walls works the same way, wall by wall, with the doors and windows taken out.

Can I get a metal roof takeoff from an EagleView report?

Yes. Upload the EagleView report PDF or XML data export to RoofCoil Takeoff and pick the panel profile and coil width. It builds the roof in 3D from the report, lays out every panel, and returns the cutlist, panel layout, installation order and bill of materials on screen and as a takeoff report PDF.

Which EagleView reports work?

The EagleView Premium report PDF, the one with the Length, Pitch and Area diagrams, including older layouts and copies that carry a contractor's branding, and the EagleView XML data export. Reports without the roof diagrams don't carry enough to lay out panels.

How long does a metal roof takeoff take?

Seconds. Once the file is uploaded, a typical EagleView report is read, laid out and turned into a full takeoff report in about two to five seconds.

How do you lay out standing seam panels?

Work out the panel's coverage, then lay panels across each roof facet or wall from a starting edge, one coverage width apart. On a roof each panel runs from eave to ridge and is cut on the true slope; a panel that ends on a hip or valley is cut to the longest point of its run and trimmed to the line. On a wall the panels run plumb from the base to the top, with short pieces over doors and gable panels cut at their long side. RoofCoil Takeoff does the layout and draws every panel with its cut length.

How many standing seam panels do I need?

Divide the width the panels cover by the panel's coverage and round up. A 40 ft run is 480 in.; at 16-7/8 in. coverage (SS450 on 21 in. coil) that is 28.4, so 29 panels. Do it for each roof side or wall, then multiply by the panel length for the linear feet to order. Hips, valleys and openings change the cut lengths, which is why a full takeoff lays out every panel.

How is standing seam panel coverage worked out?

From the coil width you order. Each profile's seam takes up part of the coil, so coverage is the coil width minus the take-up. SS450 snap-lock takes up 4-1/8 in., so 21 in. coil covers 16-7/8 in.; SSQ200 2 in. mechanical seam takes up 5-13/16 in., so the same coil covers 15-3/16 in. and the roof needs more panels. PBR / R-panel is factory-formed at a fixed 36 in. coverage.

What coil width makes a 16 in. standing seam panel?

The coverage plus the profile's take-up. SS450 snap-lock takes up 4-1/8 in., so a 16 in. panel takes 20-1/8 in. coil; SSQ200 2 in. mechanical seam takes up 5-13/16 in., so it takes 21-13/16 in. coil. Check the take-up against your roll former's; the table on this page lists the ones the takeoff uses.

How much coil does a standing seam roof take?

A foot of coil for every foot of panel, at the coil width you roll: the panel linear feet are the coil linear feet. To estimate from square feet, multiply the area by the coil multiplier, the coil width divided by the coverage. 21 in. coil at 16-7/8 in. coverage is 1.244, so 1,000 sq ft of roof takes about 1,244 sq ft of coil before waste.

Which panel profiles does it figure?

Seven: SS450 and SSQ675 snap-lock; SS100, SS150 and SSQ200 mechanical seam; FF100 slotted flange; and PBR / R-panel at 36 in. coverage. Each carries its seam take-up and clip, so the panel count and the clip order follow the profile you pick.

Does the takeoff include trim and accessories?

Yes. Eave trim and cleat, rake trim, ridge and hip cap, W-valley and offset cleat, headwall, parapet, pitch transition and step flashing come out in 10 ft sticks with 6 in. laps, each drawn as the part the shop bends with its girth and the 4 × 10 flat sheets the order takes. Z-closures, clips by the box, screws, butyl tape, color-matched sealant, synthetic underlayment, cap nails and ice and water shield for the valleys are on the list too. Panel waste and accessory waste are set separately. A trim map shows where each part goes: every edge drawn in the color of its trim, with the parts that go on it named in the key.

How many flat sheets does the trim take?

It depends on each part's girth, the width of flat metal it is bent from. A 4 x 10 sheet is 48 in. wide, so it gives 48 divided by the girth, rounded down, pieces 10 ft long: a 12 in. girth gives 4 pieces a sheet, a 16 in. girth 3. The roof takeoff draws every trim part with its girth and adds up the 4 x 10 sheets the order takes; wall trim is counted in 10 ft sticks, run by run.

Can it take off just the metal on a commercial roof?

Yes. When an EagleView report measures a whole commercial building, flat roofs and parapets included, the takeoff covers the sections steep enough for metal panels, 3/12 and up, by default. You can add or leave out any section, and the trim where the metal meets a flat roof or a parapet stays in the takeoff.

Can it take off metal wall panels?

Yes. The wall takeoff lays out vertical wall panels, standing seam or PBR, on walls you enter from the drawings or the field: eave walls, gable ends, shed walls and the strip of a taller wall above a lower roof, with their doors, windows, overhead doors and corners. It draws every wall with its panels and gives the cut list, wall trim in 10 ft sticks, closures, screws, sealant, house wrap and a wall report PDF.

How does the wall takeoff handle doors and windows?

Enter them by tag or size: 3068 is a 3'-0" by 6'-8" door, 14x14 an overhead door. A panel the full width of a door or overhead door becomes a short piece over it; panels at a window are ordered full length and cut around it on site. Openings come off the net wall area, and each gets its trim: J-trim at window heads, sills and jambs, door and overhead door trim at heads and jambs, and sealant around every opening.

It checks itself against the report. It compares its facet count, total roof area and eave, rake, ridge, hip and valley lengths with the totals EagleView prints, and shows each check as pass or fail. A takeoff that doesn't cross-check is marked not verified on screen and on every page of the PDF, so nothing gets ordered from it without a second look.
