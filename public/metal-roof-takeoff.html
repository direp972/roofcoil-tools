In short

RoofCoil Takeoff reads an EagleView roof report — the PDF or the XML data export — and builds the whole metal roof takeoff from it: a cut length for every standing seam or PBR panel, a drawing of where each panel goes, the order to install them in, and a bill of materials for clips, screws, trim, closures, sealants and underlayment. It draws every trim piece as the part the shop bends and maps where each one goes, checks the roof it built against the totals printed in the report, and hands you everything as a takeoff report PDF. It takes seconds, and it's free with a RoofCoil account.

What's in every takeoff

Panel cutlist

Every panel's cut length to the inch, grouped by length, with the panel count, total linear feet and square feet for the order.

Panels layout & drawings

Every panel drawn where it lands with its cut length on it, plus isometric, dimension and slope drawings of the roof, on screen and in the PDF.

Installation order

A numbered start marker and arrow on each facet, and each facet's panels listed in the order they go on, so the crew pulls the right panel next.

Bill of materials

Clips by SKU and box, clip and trim screws, eave, rake, ridge and valley trim in 10 ft sticks, Z-closures, butyl, sealant, underlayment and ice & water.

Measurement checks

Facet count, roof area and every edge total compared with the figures EagleView prints, each marked pass or fail before anything is ordered.

Trim from flat sheet

Each trim line drawn as the part the shop bends — drip edge, cleats, rake, ridge and hip cap, Z-closure, W-valley — to your pitch and seam height, with its girth, pieces per 4 × 10 sheet and the sheets the order takes.

Trim map

Every edge that takes trim drawn in its own color — eave, rake, ridge, hip, valley, headwall, sidewall, pitch break — with a key naming the parts that go on it, so the crew knows where each piece goes. On screen and in the PDF.

Commercial roof sections

When a report measures a whole building, flat roofs and parapets included, the takeoff covers just the metal — sections at 3/12 and up — and you can add or leave out any section.

Compare options

The same roof in up to five options — another profile, another coil width — with panels, linear feet and every material line side by side.

Report history

Every takeoff saved to your account with its address, panel and square footage, searchable, so you can reopen it or run the measurement again.

Panel systems it figures

The takeoff carries the clip SKUs, box counts and seam take-ups for each profile, so the panel count and the clip order follow the system you pick.

Snap-lock

  • SS450 · 1.5" snap-lock, the popular residential profile
  • SSQ675 · 1.75" snap-lock, a taller seam line
  • US-150LS · 1.5" Lok-Seam, no field seaming
  • US-175LS · 1.75" Lok-Seam with concealed clips

Mechanical seam

  • SS100 · 1" double-lock, low profile
  • SS150 · 1.5" for architectural runs
  • SSQ200 · 2", the commercial workhorse, down to 2:12
  • US-150 · 1.5" single or double lock
  • US-200 · 2" for long, low-slope commercial runs

Flange & exposed fastener

  • FF100 · 1" snap-lock, slotted fastener flange, no clips
  • US-100NS · 1" nail strip, roof or siding
  • PBR / R-panel · 36" coverage, screw-down over solid deck or open purlins

Roll-formed panels are figured from the coil width you order: coverage = coil width − the profile's seam take-up. SS450 takes up 4-1/8", so 21" coil covers 16-7/8"; SSQ200 takes up 5-13/16", so the same coil covers 15-3/16" and the roof needs more panels. Compare profiles in the panel lineup or read snap-lock vs mechanical seam.

Checked against the report before you order

An EagleView report prints its own totals: the number of facets, the roof area, and the length of every eave, rake, ridge, hip and valley. The takeoff rebuilds the roof in 3D from the report's diagrams, lays the panels on that roof, and then compares what it built with those printed totals. Each comparison shows as pass or fail.

When the checks pass, the cut lengths come from a roof that agrees with the measurement. When they don't, you still get the takeoff, but it's marked not verified on screen and stamped on every page of the PDF, and the RoofCoil team is told so the reader can be fixed. A wrong number caught on screen costs nothing; a wrong number caught on the roof costs a reorder.

Cut lengths are measured on the true slope of each facet, panel by panel. A panel that ends on a hip or valley is cut to the longest point of its run, rounded up to the inch, and trimmed to the line on the roof, so the cutlist matches what goes up rather than a squares-times-waste guess. If your crew measures trim on site, how to measure metal roof trim covers the same runs.

Frequently asked

What is a metal roof takeoff?

A metal roof takeoff is the list of everything it takes to build the roof, worked out from its measurements: how many panels and each one's cut length, plus the trim, clips, fasteners, closures, sealants and underlayment that go with them. On a standing seam roof the panels have to be laid out facet by facet, because every hip and valley changes the cut lengths.

Can I get a metal roof takeoff from an EagleView report?

Yes. Upload the EagleView report PDF or XML data export to RoofCoil Takeoff and pick the panel profile and coil width. It builds the roof in 3D from the report, lays out every panel, and returns the cutlist, panel layout, installation order and bill of materials on screen and as a takeoff report PDF.

Which EagleView reports work?

The EagleView Premium report PDF, the one with the Length, Pitch and Area diagrams, including older layouts and copies that carry a contractor's branding, and the EagleView XML data export. Reports without the roof diagrams don't carry enough to lay out panels.

How long does a metal roof takeoff take?

Seconds. Once the file is uploaded, a typical EagleView report is read, laid out and turned into a full takeoff report in about two to five seconds.

How is standing seam panel coverage worked out?

From the coil width you order. Each profile's seam takes up part of the coil, so coverage is the coil width minus the take-up. SS450 snap-lock takes up 4-1/8 in., so 21 in. coil covers 16-7/8 in.; SSQ200 2 in. mechanical seam takes up 5-13/16 in., so the same coil covers 15-3/16 in. and the roof needs more panels. PBR / R-panel is factory-formed at a fixed 36 in. coverage.

Does the takeoff include trim and accessories?

Yes. Eave trim and cleat, rake trim, ridge and hip cap, W-valley and offset cleat, headwall, parapet, pitch transition and step flashing come out in 10 ft sticks with 6 in. laps, each drawn as the part the shop bends with its girth and the 4 × 10 flat sheets the order takes. Z-closures, clips by the box, screws, butyl tape, color-matched sealant, synthetic underlayment, cap nails and ice and water shield for the valleys are on the list too. Panel waste and accessory waste are set separately. A trim map shows where each part goes: every edge drawn in the color of its trim, with the parts that go on it named in the key.

Can it take off just the metal on a commercial roof?

Yes. When an EagleView report measures a whole commercial building, flat roofs and parapets included, the takeoff covers the sections steep enough for metal panels, 3/12 and up, by default. You can add or leave out any section, and the trim where the metal meets a flat roof or a parapet stays in the takeoff.

Does RoofCoil Takeoff price the job?

No. The takeoff gives quantities, not prices. Price the panels and trim in RoofCoil's Panel & Trim app, which prices the run from the shop nearest you, or send the PDF to a listed fabricator for a quote.

Is RoofCoil Takeoff free?

Yes. It needs a free RoofCoil account, which you can create on this page in about a minute. It's the same login as the Panel & Trim app, and every takeoff you run is saved to your report history so you can open it again later.

What if my measurement report isn't from EagleView?

Upload it anyway. The takeoff only reads the formats it was built for, so it won't guess at a RoofScope, Hover or other report. The file goes straight to the RoofCoil team, who add readers for the formats contractors send. You can also email it to orders@roofcoil.com.

Can I compare panel profiles on the same roof?

Yes. Add up to four more options, each a different profile, coil width or PBR fastening, and the takeoff reads the roof once and lays out every option, showing panel counts, linear feet and every material line side by side with a PDF for each.

How accurate is the takeoff?

It checks itself against the report. It compares its facet count, total roof area and eave, rake, ridge, hip and valley lengths with the totals EagleView prints, and shows each check as pass or fail. A takeoff that doesn't cross-check is marked not verified on screen and on every page of the PDF, so nothing gets ordered from it without a second look.
